[curious] People say caviar costs more than gold. [mysterious] It doesn't. Gram for gram, gold still costs over ten times more. [amazed] But one small tin of beluga caviar can take eighteen years to make. [short pause] I'll show you why, step by step. The answer is at the end. [warm] Share this video and subscribe, so everyone sees how hard this job really is.

[curious] Caviar is the salted eggs of a fish called sturgeon. [calm] Today, most of it comes from farms. [amazed] And the biggest producer isn't Russia or Iran. It's a lake in China.

[excited] It all starts with baby sturgeon, smaller than your finger. [calm] They grow in big round tanks, with cold, clean water flowing all the time. [curious] Then comes the hard part: waiting. [amazed] A Siberian sturgeon needs about seven years. A beluga needs up to eighteen. [warm] If you're enjoying this, hit like.

[mysterious] Farmers check each female with an ultrasound, to see if her eggs are ready. [amazed] At seven years, only one or two fish out of ten are ready. The rest go back to the tank for another year. [calm] When she's ready, the eggs are gently sieved, rinsed, and lightly salted. [curious] Some farms can even take the eggs without harming the fish. [excited] Then they're packed into small tins, kept ice cold, and shipped to restaurants around the world.

[curious] So why is caviar so expensive? [mysterious] Because you may wait up to eighteen years for one fish. [amazed] And only about eight percent of her weight becomes caviar. [calm] That's years of feed, cold water and care, in every tiny spoon.

[excited] Have you ever tried caviar? Yes or no, tell me in the comments. [warm] And that's why caviar is one of the most expensive foods on Earth.
