[curious] Why is lobster so expensive? [mysterious] One big reason: almost every lobster you eat is still caught in the wild. [amazed] Because to farm them, every single lobster needs its own private room. [short pause] I'll show you why, step by step. The answer is at the end. [warm] Share this video and subscribe, so everyone sees how hard this job really is.

[curious] Here's what a lobster farm looks like. [calm] In Norway, lobsters are raised on land, in heated seawater. [warm] And in Cornwall, England, a hatchery has released hundreds of thousands of baby lobsters into the sea.

[excited] It all starts with a mother lobster carrying eggs, brought in by local fishermen. [amazed] When the eggs hatch, tiny larvae drift through the water. [calm] In cone-shaped tanks, the water keeps spinning, so they never sink to the bottom. [curious] They shed their shell three times. [amazed] By stage four, they finally look like tiny lobsters. [warm] If you're enjoying this, hit like.

[mysterious] And right at that moment, each one gets its own room. [calm] Clean seawater flows through every cell, at around sixty-five to seventy degrees. [curious] In cold water, a lobster almost stops growing in winter. [excited] In warm water, it grows all year round. [amazed] One Norwegian farm even signed a deal with a data center. The water that cools the computers will warm the lobsters.

[calm] They eat a special feed. [amazed] About one pound of feed grows about one pound of lobster. [excited] Still, it takes about two years to reach plate size, around eight inches and ten ounces. [warm] That's a lot of time, space and warm water, for one lobster roll.

[curious] So why can't lobsters share a tank, like salmon do? [mysterious] Because lobsters are cannibals. Put them together, and they eat each other. [amazed] Raised together, about nine out of ten babies from one mother can be lost this way. [calm] That's why lobster farming is so rare, and lobster is never cheap.

[excited] Would you pay more for a farmed lobster? Tell me in the comments. [warm] And that's one big reason lobster is so expensive.
