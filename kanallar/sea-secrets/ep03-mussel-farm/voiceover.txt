[mysterious] Nobody ever fed the mussels on your plate. [excited] They grew fat hanging from a rope in the sea. [short pause] Here's how mussels are farmed, step by step. [warm] Share this video and subscribe, so everyone can see how much work goes into it.

[curious] Whether you eat them steamed in white wine or with a pile of fries, many of these mussels start life the same way. [calm] From Prince Edward Island in Canada to Galicia in Spain, they're grown on ropes in the sea. [warm] Some are gathered from wild beds, but many come from sea farms like this one. [curious] So how do you farm a mussel?

[excited] It starts with a single rope hanging in the water. [mysterious] Millions of tiny mussel larvae drift through the sea, almost invisible. [amazed] They grab onto the rope all by themselves, sometimes tens of thousands on a single meter! [warm] Give this video a like if it's useful.

[excited] Once they grow a little, farmers pack them into mesh socks. The socks hang from long lines, held up by floats. [curious] So who feeds them? [mysterious] Here's the secret. [amazed] A mussel pumps seawater through its body and catches the tiny plankton inside. A mussel just five centimeters long can filter five liters of water an hour, more than a gallon! So the sea itself is their food.

[excited] In one to two years, a baby the size of a fingernail grows into a full mussel. [amazed] A single meter of rope can carry almost eight kilos of mussels, about seventeen pounds. [excited] Then the lines are pulled onto the boat, and the mussels are cleaned and sorted by size. [warm] And finally, they're steamed with garlic and white wine, and served with a pile of crispy fries.

[excited] Tell us in the comments which seafood we should explain next. [mysterious] Because right now, somewhere in the sea, millions of mussels are growing on a rope.
